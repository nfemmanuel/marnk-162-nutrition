## Interview findings

- **Students and macros.** A teammate who interviewed friends in Poland found that students, even those living alone, mostly do not care about macros, and many get school meals. Only gym-goers care. This raises the question of whether a macro-focused app suits students.
- **Calories.** A few people said seeing calories led to unhealthy eating habits. The proposal was to let users hide calories and show a proxy instead, such as Germany's Nutri-Score, an A to E grade.
- **NF's interviews.** One interviewee cared deeply about counting calories and others want to track loosely, so calorie tracking would be a setting. People want variety from the same four or five ingredients, and everyone wants to know what they can make with what is in the fridge. Proposed direction is a simple grocery and recipe app, configurable toward goals like macros, with preferences.

## Discussion

- **Recipe data versus an LLM.** Ransith found the recipe APIs hold basic recipes and cannot handle preferences such as high protein. An LLM would do better but needs an API key. NF noted an LLM draws on stored data anyway and a local store answers faster, and good indexing with search tags and ingredient-to-macro links may close the quality gap. A teammate suggested starting with a small recipe dataset matched against user input.
- **Agreed approach.** Start with a small recipe dataset, then add indexing. Tagging ingredients with macros can start before the walking skeleton is done. Spices can be left out, and staples like pasta, chicken and beef need proper tags.
