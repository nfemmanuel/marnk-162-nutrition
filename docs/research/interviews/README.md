## Patterns so far

- **Nobody keeps a tracker updated by hand.** All five people we asked (P1–P5) quit one or wouldn't start. The only logging that lasted took no effort: a smart scale (P2) or a photo sent to ChatGPT (P4).
- **Boredom isn't what ends batch cooking.** Only P6 said so. For the others the food runs out or stops being fresh by day 4 (P3, P4), or the fridge is too small (P5), and P2 preps 12 portions a week without getting bored.
- **Food goes off even when people know their fridge.** Vegetables, herbs and a whole meal prep got thrown out (P3, P4, P7), and one missing ingredient turned a 20-minute dish into a 30-minute round trip to the shop (P2).
- **"Cook with what you have" came up without us asking.** P3 described the idea unprompted, P5 wants to "buy rice once and use it in five different dishes", and P1's family usually cooks "what's in the fridge".
- **Cost isn't always first.** Time and effort (P6, P7) and health (P4) came up as often as price (P3, P5). P6 wants high-protein meals.
- **ChatGPT is the competitor to beat.** P4 replaced two paid trackers with it, and P5 wouldn't pay for an app because they can just ask a chatbot.
- **Calories should be optional.** Two people (P3, P4) said counting calories is bad for them.

## Summaries

Every summary has the same fields. "Not covered" means the interview didn't get to that topic.

### P1. Karolina, 1 Oct 2026

- **Who:** a student who lives with their parents. In person, at home. Group: neither.
- **Asked:** do you know the nutrition you get, how do you decide what to eat, would an app help, have you ever had a food app.
- **Cost or macros:** neither. The parents cook; they suggest options, but it's usually "what's in the fridge".
- **Batch cooking:** never needed to. The family cooks for 3–4 people, so it already cooks in bulk.
- **Fridge:** yes, they check it when they're about to cook.
- **Keeping an app updated:** too much work, and no point: the parents cook and stick to recipes they trust.
- **Device:** not covered.
- **Stand-out:** they go back to recipes they know and rarely look for new ones.
- **For the app:** an "I have it in the fridge" option.

### P2. NF, 4 Oct 2026

- **Who:** not a student, possibly on a budget. Oakland. Group: budget planner (possibly).
- **Asked:** the last time they ate at home, ordered out or meal-prepped; what's in their fridge; apps they've used; how they plan their shopping.
- **Cost or macros:** not covered.
- **Batch cooking:** meal-preps 12 portions a week and doesn't get bored of it. Skips cooking about twice a week when they don't feel like it.
- **Fridge:** one missing ingredient turned a 20-minute dish into a 15-minute walk each way.
- **Keeping an app updated:** logs food only when it's effortless (a smart scale that saves meals). Otherwise: "I'm lazy, so I don't stick to it."
- **Device:** not covered.
- **Stand-out:** 12 portions a week with no boredom, the opposite of our guess.
- **For the app:** meal prep is the real signal; onboarding has to be fast; the app should know what's missing before you start cooking.

### P3. Alex, 6 Oct 2026

- **Who:** a woman who lived alone in the US and is now back in Berlin with family; most answers are about when she cooked for herself. In person, at her home. Group: neither.
- **Asked:** 17 questions on her week, last food shop, spending, batch cooking, fridge and food waste, tracking apps, shopping list, and where she decides what to eat.
- **Cost or macros:** cost first. About €80–100 a week, and she puts pricier treats back. She has started glancing at calories and thinks that's bad for her.
- **Batch cooking:** still does it. 2–3 dishes cooked at the end of the week last 4 days, 5 at most. By then they aren't fresh and are less appealing, but she finishes them to avoid waste.
- **Fridge:** knows it well when she shops for herself, and checks before writing a list. Vegetables and herbs still go bad, because turning them into a dish means buying more ingredients ("it's like this snowball starts").
- **Keeping an app updated:** tried MyFitnessPal for one day. The numbers scared her, the useful features are paid, and home-cooked food can't be logged without a scale. A grocery list in Notes was too slow. Never paid for a food app.
- **Device:** phone. Recipes come from Instagram and TikTok, saved and sent to herself in DMs.
- **Stand-out:** without being prompted, she described our idea: recipes from what you already have, or ones that need only a couple of extra ingredients. She also builds her list in the Rewe or Kaufland app's cart to see the total cost.
- **For the app:** cook with what you have plus one or two extras, aimed at leftover vegetables and herbs; show the shopping list's estimated cost; save recipes from a link; calories optional or hidden by default; plan batches for 4 days, not 7.

### P4. Alex, 8 Oct 2026

- **Who:** a working woman who lives with a roommate; they don't share food. In person, in a gym common area. Group: eating out less.
- **Asked:** the same 17 questions as P3.
- **Cost or macros:** neither. She picks food that looks healthy, prefers organic, and checks ingredients and macros in Yuka. She spends about €300 a week, mostly on delivery, and accepts that.
- **Batch cooking:** cooks one dish every Sunday. It runs out by day 4 and there's no time to cook again, so days 5–7 are delivery. Time, not boredom.
- **Fridge:** listed about ten items from memory, but knew she was missing one. Threw out a whole meal prep last week because she didn't feel like eating it.
- **Keeping an app updated:** paid for Lifesum and a MyFitnessPal trial, then quit both; logging every dish in a separate app is "too big of a commitment". Now she sends ChatGPT a photo, because rough numbers are enough.
- **Device:** laptop and phone, at the office, deciding to order lunch.
- **Stand-out:** a photo sent to ChatGPT replaced two paid tracking apps.
- **For the app:** cover days 5–7 after a batch runs out (a second quick dish, or a 15-minute meal); any logging has to be as quick as a photo; she orders groceries on Flink, so a list could feed an online order; calories optional, not the centre of the app.

### P5. NF, 11 Oct 2026

- **Who:** a recent graduate looking for work. In person. Group: not noted.
- **Asked:** the same as P2, plus grocery spending, what they'd want first in an app, and whether they'd pay.
- **Cost or macros:** cost first. Eating out would be $700–1,000 a month; groceries are $250–300.
- **Batch cooking:** stopped because the fridge is too small.
- **Fridge:** not covered. They hate leftover ingredients.
- **Keeping an app updated:** dropped Fitbit logging because typing in calories every day is "a lot".
- **Device:** not covered.
- **Stand-out:** wouldn't pay for the app, because they can just ask ChatGPT.
- **For the app:** the first thing they want is to "buy rice once and use it in five different dishes", cheap and with few ingredients; we have to be better than asking a chatbot.

### P6. Mark, 14 Oct 2026

- **Who:** an anonymous respondent, not a Minervan, who plans meals and wants to eat out less. Remote form. Group: both.
- **Asked:** 11 form questions, mostly multiple choice, about their last meal choice, batch cooking, their fridge, apps, and what would help.
- **Cost or macros:** time and effort mattered most. They also want meals that fit a high-protein diet.
- **Batch cooking:** stopped because eating the same food got boring.
- **Fridge:** checks the fridge or kitchen before choosing what to cook.
- **Keeping an app updated:** has never used a meal, calorie, grocery or pantry app.
- **Device:** phone.
- **Stand-out:** the problem is time, not knowing about nutrition. What would help: "The perfect blend between time conservation, taste and a high protein diet."
- **For the app:** quick, varied, high-protein suggestions that need very little typing.

### P7. Mark, 14 Oct 2026

- **Who:** an anonymous respondent, not a Minervan, trying to eat out less. Remote form. Group: eating out less.
- **Asked:** the same form as P6.
- **Cost or macros:** neither; time and effort mattered most. Last time they ate at a restaurant because it felt easier.
- **Batch cooking:** never tried it.
- **Fridge:** often finds food only after it has gone bad.
- **Keeping an app updated:** has never used one, though "a meal guide/tracking app" is what they said would make choosing easier.
- **Device:** nothing.
- **Stand-out:** loses track of food until it spoils, but has never tried a pantry app.
- **For the app:** suggest meals from food they already have, maybe with expiry reminders; no heavy manual tracking.
